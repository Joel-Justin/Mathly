Compound Interest

Compound interest adds interest to the amount already in the account, so the total grows faster over time.

Key idea

Interest is calculated on the increasing balance, not only the original amount.

Examples

  • £200 at 5% annual interest becomes £210 after one year.
  • After two years, it grows again on the new total.
  • Use a repeated multiplier for each year.

Quick rule

Use the formula A = P(1 + r)^n to calculate the final amount after n years.

Practice

  • Calculate £500 at 4% annually for 3 years.
  • Compare simple interest with compound interest.
  • Explain why the amount grows faster over time.
