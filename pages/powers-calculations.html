Powers & Calculations

Powers show repeated multiplication, and they help us work efficiently with very large and very small numbers. This topic also introduces roots, standard form, and useful laws of indices.

What is a power?

A power shows how many times a number is multiplied by itself. For example, 3² = 3 × 3 = 9 and 5³ = 5 × 5 × 5 = 125. The small number is called the index or exponent.

Examples

  • 2⁴ = 16
  • 10² = 100
  • √49 = 7
  • 3 × 10⁴ = 30000

Quick rule

When multiplying powers with the same base, add the exponents. When dividing, subtract them.

Roots

A square root is the opposite of squaring a number. Since 7² = 49, the square root of 49 is 7. Roots are useful for solving equations and finding side lengths of squares.

Standard form

Standard form is useful for writing very large or very small numbers clearly. For example, 4.2 × 10³ = 4200 and 3.5 × 10⁻² = 0.035. Scientists and engineers often use standard form when working with large distances or tiny measurements.
