Measurement

Measurement helps us compare length, time, area, volume, and capacity in real situations. It is used in cooking, building, sport, travel, and science.

Units of length

Common metric units include millimetres, centimetres, metres, and kilometres. We choose a unit depending on the size of what we are measuring. For example, a pencil might be measured in centimetres, while a road trip is measured in kilometres.

Examples

  • 1 m = 100 cm
  • 1 km = 1000 m
  • 1 litre = 1000 millilitres
  • Perimeter of a rectangle = 2(l + w)

Quick rule

Convert using multiplying or dividing by 10, 100, or 1000 depending on the unit.

Perimeter and area

Perimeter is the total distance around a shape, while area measures the space inside a shape. For a rectangle, area is calculated as length × width. For a square, area is side × side.

Volume and capacity

Volume tells us how much space a 3D object takes up. A cuboid’s volume is length × width × height. Capacity is often used with liquids and is measured in millilitres or litres.

Practice

  • Convert 3.5 m to cm
  • Find the area of a rectangle 6 cm by 4 cm
  • Find the perimeter of a square with side 7 cm
  • Convert 2500 ml to litres
  • Find the volume of a box with sides 3 cm, 4 cm, and 5 cm
