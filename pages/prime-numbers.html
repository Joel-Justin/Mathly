Prime Numbers

A prime number is a whole number greater than 1 that has exactly two factors: 1 and itself.

Examples of primes

2, 3, 5, 7, 11, and 13 are prime numbers. They cannot be divided exactly by any whole number other than 1 and themselves.

Examples

  • 7 is prime because its only factors are 1 and 7
  • 11 is prime because it has exactly two factors
  • 9 is not prime because it has factors 1, 3, and 9

Quick rule

If a number has more than two factors, it is not prime.

Prime numbers in number theory

Prime numbers are important because every whole number can be built from prime factors. They are the “building blocks” of arithmetic.
