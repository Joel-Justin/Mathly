Number & Arithmetic

Numbers are the foundation of maths, and arithmetic helps us compute with accuracy and confidence. This topic covers place value, operations, factors, and the rules that keep calculations consistent.

Place value

Every digit in a number has a place value. In 473, the 4 is in the hundreds place, the 7 is in the tens place, and the 3 is in the ones place. Place value helps us read, compare, and round numbers correctly.

Examples

  • 45 + 18 = 63
  • 92 - 27 = 65
  • 6 × 7 = 42
  • 48 ÷ 6 = 8

Quick rule

Use BODMAS: Brackets, Orders, Division, Multiplication, Addition, Subtraction.

Negative numbers

Negative numbers are less than zero and appear on the number line to the left of zero. Example: -3 < 2. In real life, negative numbers are used for temperatures below freezing and balances in debt.

Factors, multiples, and primes

A factor is a whole number that divides exactly into another number. A multiple is a number in that times table. A prime number has exactly two factors: 1 and itself. For example, 7 is prime, while 12 has factors 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 12.
